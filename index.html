What Do Vision-Language Model Descriptions Make of a Cultural Object?

A cultural object, many faces.
Following what survives a description.

01 / The question

Does a description
keep the object,
or only its kind?

A fluent description can name the right culture while losing the particular object. Polytropos turns each unchanged description back into an image and compares it with the original photograph.

We follow what survives this passage: the museum-recorded culture, the details of an individual object, and the distance between them.

374 objects from the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Getty. Five vision-language models. A matched baseline of museum-authored descriptions.

The evaluation

One object.
A chain of interpretations.

The generator receives only the unchanged description. The recogniser sees only image pixels. Museum culture references are used for scoring.

I.

Look & describe

A model describes a museum object from its photograph and available catalogue context.

II.

Keep the words

Five of fifteen models pass the language gate. Their descriptions remain verbatim throughout reuse.

III.

Render again

The same fixed generator and wrapper turn every model and curator description into an image.

IV.

Recognise culture

An image-only recogniser predicts culture; scoring compares it with the object's recorded attribution.

The paired control: the same recogniser also sees the original photograph. The comparison tracks cultures kept, lost or gained on the same objects. Refusals remain in the fixed denominator.

02 / Benchmark results

The leaderboard

Published results · fixed evaluation protocol
Model descriptions → reconstructed images — 95% bootstrap interval

Cultural-attribution recoverability is distinct from overall description quality and preservation of the specific object. Comparisons condition on one fixed generator, recogniser and execution. Point estimates do not imply statistically significant rank differences.

What the aggregate conceals

More recognisable as a culture.
Less recognisable as itself.

Model reconstructions reach 54.55–59.09% culture agreement, compared with 52.14% for original photographs. Yet only 0.49% of reconstructed images elicit a specific-object identity claim.

On the same 230 objects, models lose recorded cultures at rates similar to curators but gain more. Small aggregate gains coexist with 16–21% of objects changing recognition outcome.

Museum-authored descriptions © J. Paul Getty Trust. CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise noted; Getty source-record terms apply. All 230 matched human descriptions are from Getty records.

Both corpora cover the same 230 objects. AI: 1,150 descriptions, 59,712 retained content tokens. Museum-authored: 230 descriptions, 13,165 retained content tokens. Both use spaCy part-of-speech tagging on the original sentences, retaining nouns, proper nouns and adjectives; identical English stopwords, number words and weak-framing exclusions apply. No stemming or lemmatisation. Rates are per 10,000 retained content tokens. Up to 90 words are displayed per cloud. Font sizes are scaled independently within each cloud, so compare the normalised rates, not sizes across clouds. Colours are decorative; lexical usage does not measure description quality.
